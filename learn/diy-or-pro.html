DIY or Pro? When to Send Your Card In

Some card care is genuinely safe to do at home. Some of it is delicate enough that one wrong pass makes things worse forever. Here's how we'd decide — honestly, including the times the answer is "don't send it to us."

PokéSpa · Updated August 31, 2026

We run a restoration service, so you might expect this page to end with "send us everything." It won't. Plenty of Pokémon card care is well within reach at your kitchen table, and our guides will walk you through it for free. What follows is the same triage we use on our own bench: what's safe for you, what's safer with us, and what nobody should do at all.

Start with the risk, not the damage

The damage your card already has is done. It's stable. It isn't getting worse sitting in a sleeve. The real question is different: what damage might you add trying to fix it?

Dust adds nothing when you blow it off. But rub a vintage matte card and you can lift ink that will never come back. That's the whole framework. Reversible, low-force, add-nothing methods are DIY territory. Anything that presses, rubs, wets, or reshapes a delicate surface is where a careful hand — and the willingness to stop — earns its keep.

The value test. The more a card is worth — in money or in memory — the stronger the case for a professional. Not because the work is magic, but because a DIY mistake is usually irreversible. On a bulk card, a mistake costs you a bulk card. On the card your dad gave you, it costs you something you can't reorder.

What's genuinely safe to do yourself

These are low-risk because they add nothing and force nothing:

  • Loose dust and grit. An air blower lifts it off without a single touch. Always the first move.
  • Light surface grime on modern gloss cards. A dry microfiber cloth, feather-light, on the glossy coating modern cards have. Our cleaning guide covers the technique.
  • Storage upgrades. Sleeves, toploaders, a cool dry shelf. The highest-return card care there is, and zero risk.
  • Gentle humidity de-warping of a cupped card. Slow, patient, and community-accepted — because it's reversible and adds nothing to the card. Rushing it is the only way to get it wrong.

If your card's problem is on that list, you don't need us. Truly.

What belongs with a pro

These are the jobs where the margin for error is thin and the failure mode is permanent:

  • Creases and bends. Paper fibers are broken. Working them takes patience and restraint, and overworking them shows.
  • Stains and residue. What lifts a stain from one card's surface can drive it deeper into another's, or take the finish with it.
  • Vintage matte stock. Older cards have no protective gloss. The print sits right at the surface, and rubbing damages it. This is the single most common DIY casualty we hear about.
  • Wax or gum residue on vintage cards. It's stuck to an unprotected surface. Scraping or scrubbing it off takes ink with it.
  • Edge and corner work. Small areas, high visibility, no room to blend a mistake.
  • Anything on a card of real value. See the value test above. Even a "safe" job is worth a second opinion when the card matters.

None of this means the work is impossible for you. It means the first attempt shouldn't happen on a card you care about — and the card you care about is the only reason to attempt it.

What nobody should do — not you, not us

Trimming edges. Recoloring worn spots. Filling dings. Adding gloss. That's not restoration — it's card doctoring, done to make a card pass as something it isn't. We don't offer it, we won't quote it, and we'd talk you out of trying it yourself. The line between honest care and deception matters to this whole hobby, and we wrote a full guide on exactly where it sits.

If grading is the goal, don't treat the card — by anyone, including us. PSA, BGS and CGC policies treat cleaning solutions, added gloss, scratch removal and pressing as alteration, and a restored card is an altered card. Restoration is for cards you want to keep and enjoy honestly — not a step on the way to a grade. Read our grading guide before you do anything else.

What actually happens if you send it in

No mystery, no upsell. You send a few photos. We look at them and tell you the truth: what can improve, what can't, and exactly what we'd do — including "nothing," if that's the right answer. If you go ahead, the work is careful and conservative, and it comes back with full documentation: before-and-after photos and a written list of exactly what was done, so the card's history stays honest for whoever holds it next. The assessment costs nothing either way.

That's the whole decision. Safe and reversible: do it yourself, with our guides open. Delicate or valuable: let someone assess it first. Doctoring: nobody. And if you're not sure which bucket your card falls in — that's precisely what the free assessment is for.
